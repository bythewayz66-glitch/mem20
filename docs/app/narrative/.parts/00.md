## 4. Twenty-two agents, one hour, and two absences

On 1 November the owner issued a directive that is, in this chronicle's reading, the single most
important document of the window. It is 192 lines, it names ten verified gaps, and it assigns
twenty-two agents to close them with an acceptance criterion for each.

It is important for two reasons. The first is that it is the clearest statement in the repository of
the program's evidence standard:

> **Standing rule (owner, restated):** a deliverable exists only as a **commit SHA on a pushed
> branch** or a **permanent URL committed under `docs/`**. Chat-only claims do not count and will be
> scored as absent.

The second is that it was scored, honestly, an hour later, and the score is in the repository.

### 4.1 The ten gaps

The directive's §2 register is worth reproducing in full, because it is the best single snapshot of
what was wrong on 1 November:

| # | Gap | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | `Tests` check is RED on main | 14 check-runs on `2ea44922`: 13 success, 1 failure |
| G2 | 13 merged-but-undeleted branches | 13 non-`main` heads, all ancestors of main |
| G3 | Launch artifacts stale on version | `docs/index.html` and `mem20.com` both carry `270 tools` ×8, `27 domains` ×8, `v3.0.0` ×4 |
| G4 | Tag is 63 commits stale | `compare/v3.1.1...main` → ahead_by 63, behind_by 0 |
| G5 | PyPI 404s | `mem20`, `mem20-mcp`, `mem20-sdk` all 404 |
| G6 | Three domains unregistered | `.org` / `.store` / `.info` return GitHub 9,115-byte 404 |
| G7 | Chronicle is 5 days stale | `serial-2026-10-27.md` is the only narrative file |

G7 is this serial's own row, and it is worth recording what it asked for, because it is the only
place in the repository where the chronicle's obligations are written down by someone other than the
chronicle:

> **22. Mem20-naration** — G7
> - Bring the chronicle series current. `serial-2026-10-27.md` stops at frontier `e7758bd`; publish
>   the next instalments covering: main `2ea44922`, 270 tools / 27 domains, `VERSION` 3.1.0 + tag
>   v3.1.1, the at-risk convergence **10 → 3 → 0** (`docs/ops/branch-inventory-2026-10-31.md` §196),
>   and the retirement of the Sidney/gabe infra.
> - **Acceptance:** new files under `docs/app/narrative/` committed on a branch, each pinned to a
>   real baseline SHA and citing the command behind every figure.

Parts one and two of this serial did exactly that, and the roster score credits them:

> | 22 | Mem20-naration | **DELIVERED** | `feba039c68c76bf724896d00153e30e6e744c693` | `docs/app/narrative/serial-2026-11-01.md` + `-11-01b.md` + publication note (`1376fb47`) |

Note the SHA. The roster credits `feba039c` for the two posts and `1376fb47` for the publication
note. `1376fb47` is the commit whose subject says *"main moved to 3.1.2 mid-run"* — the correct
version, before the 18 November sweep rewrote it. The roster score is therefore a second, independent
record that the note originally said 3.1.2, and it is the record that makes §1 provable rather than
merely argued.

### 4.2 The score

The directive was issued at 17:22:57Z. The roster was scored at 18:24:00Z, sixty-one minutes later,
by Mem20-admin, and the scoring method is stated so it cannot be softened later:

> **Strictness rule (stated so it cannot be softened later):** a row is DELIVERED only if the
> artifact is carried by a commit whose committer date is **after** the roster commit
> (`17:22:57Z`). Pre-roster trees on `main` — e.g. `docs/finance/CLAIMS_CHECK_H18.md` from 2026-10-31
> — are work from an **earlier** round and are **not** credited here. Crediting them would be the
> same error as crediting a chat claim.

The result:

| | Count |
|---|---:|
| Rows scored | 23 |
| DELIVERED | 21 |
| ABSENT | 2 |
| Delivered with an unmet sub-criterion | 2 |

Twenty-one of twenty-three in sixty-one minutes, with 67 commits landing on 21 refs in the window.
That is a genuinely impressive throughput, and the score document does not oversell it: it flags the
two partial deliveries rather than rounding them up.

### 4.3 The two absences

The two absences are named plainly, and the language is worth preserving because it is the register
this chronicle is trying to hold:

> **Row 7 · AI Engineer — ABSENT.** The named deliverable
> `docs/benchmarks/retrieval-gap-analysis-2026-11-01.md` is present on **no** ref. A tree-wide search
> for `retrieval-gap` / `gap-analysis` across all 21 refs returned nothing. The only post-roster
> benchmark work is row 8's registry flags, which is a different deliverable. This is not "in
> progress" — nothing was pushed.
>
> **Row 9 · Mem20 Benchmark Dataset Builder — ABSENT.** `benchmarks/results/latest.md` and
> `latest.json` are unchanged since `35500f4c` (2026-10-31 18:18 −0600), i.e. **before** the roster.
> No scored adversarial_hard or unanswerable row was published in the window. The existing
> `latest.md` still carries adversarial rows at hit rate `0.000`, which is the gap the row was meant
> to close.

Both absences are on the same gap, G9, and both are about the same thing: the benchmark rows that
would show where retrieval fails. The adversarial rows sit at hit rate `0.000` and nobody published a
scored replacement.

This matters more than a missing document usually would, because of what the project's own
engineering culture says about partial passes. `PROOF.md`'s rule — *"A partial pass is not a clean
pass"* — is the standard the chronicle is written to, and the benchmark gap is the place where the
project's own numbers most need that standard. An adversarial slice at `0.000` is not a benchmark
result; it is a measurement that the system fails a category completely, published without a
diagnosis of why.
