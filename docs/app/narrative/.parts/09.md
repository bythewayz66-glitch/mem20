So the window ends with a repository that is one check away from release-clean, and a commit in the
middle of the last day that claimed to have finished the job three checks early.

> **What broke.** Commit `7ba10cf4` is titled *"make all four red checks green on main."* Its parent
> had five red checks; the commit left three. The subject overstates the result by three checks, and
> the overstatement is in the one field a reader is most likely to trust without checking.
>
> **What we learned.** This is the same defect as the publication note in §1, in a different medium.
> There, a find-and-replace made a dated record state a false version. Here, a commit subject states
> a result the commit did not achieve. Both are claims that a reader would take at face value, and
> both are wrong in the direction of looking better. The project's own standard — *"A partial pass is
> not a clean pass"* — is exactly the standard this subject violates: five red to three red is a
> partial pass, and calling it "all green" is the failure the standard exists to prevent. **The
> subject line is a claim, and a claim about a check must be written after the check has run, not
> before.** The body of `7ba10cf4` is exemplary; the subject is the only part of it that is wrong, and
> it is the part that will be read.

### 11.4 The artifact-evidence check

The one remaining red check is worth a paragraph, because it is the check that has been red the
longest. It appears in the 16 November program status as one of three reds:

> | `artifact evidence` | 8 | [43820093456](...) |

Eight annotations. And it appears in the 1 November scoreboard's list of passing checks, which means
it was green then and is red now. The failures corpus records the rule it enforces, from 2 November:

> `85cee574|fix(ci): exempt convergence merges and gate-own changes from artifact-evidence`

So the check requires every PR to carry a 40-character SHA or a 200-verified URL, and it has been
patched at least once to exempt convergence merges. It is red at the end of the window with eight
annotations and no summary, which means the check is failing and the check-run does not say why in a
form the API exposes.

This post cannot determine the cause. What it can say is that the check that enforces the program's
central evidence rule is itself failing, and that the failure is not diagnosable from the check-run
output. That is a real limit, and §13 records it as one.

## 12. What the failures corpus says about the window

The failures corpus grew during the window, and the growth is itself a finding.

### 12.1 The corpus was stale at the frontier

Part two §9 measured the corpus and found that its newest entry was dated 2026-09-29 — a month before
the frontier. That was accurate on 1 November.

It is no longer accurate. The root `failures.md` now runs to 2026-11-16, and the entries from 4
November onward are the ones this post has been quoting throughout. The corpus did not stay stale; it
was extended, and the extension is the primary source for §6 through §11.

### 12.2 The entries that are not failures

Three entries in the window are recorded as non-failures, and the distinction is worth preserving
because it is the corpus's most disciplined habit:

> - 2026-11-04: Session completed successfully following all mem20 AGENTS.md rules. No failures
>   encountered.
> - 2026-11-14: **No new technical failures in this session.** A directory was created successfully
>   on first attempt. An existing upload frontend was already running and verified healthy (HTTP 200,
>   tailnet serve configured); the user reported it works as requested. Noted accurately: the working
>   frontend was pre-existing (not created/modified this session) to avoid misattribution.
> - 2026-11-16: **No failures encountered in this phase.** All tests pass (107 frontend, 70 server),
>   builds pass, typecheck passes.

The second entry is the important one. The agent created a directory, found an existing service
already running, and recorded that the service was **pre-existing and not created this session** —
*"to avoid misattribution."* That is an agent declining credit for work it did not do, in a document
whose only reader is the next agent. It is the single best example in the repository of the culture
the chronicle is trying to describe.

### 12.3 The entry that is a correction of the corpus itself

One entry in the window corrects an earlier entry, and it does so in the open:

> - 2026-11-04: #218's criterion-4 defect is fixed (97 shims + declared-entry-point detection), but
>   the issue also names six subsystems that have no usable CLI at all: ... Building those CLIs is
>   separate work, so #218 is fixed for the shim/CLI-detection cases and explicitly **not** claimed
>   as fully closed. NEXT ACTION: follow-up issue for the six remaining CLI-less subsystems.

*"Explicitly not claimed as fully closed"* is the sentence. An issue was fixed for the cases it
named and the fix declined to claim the whole issue, because part of it was separate work. That is
the same discipline as the roster score's two partial deliveries, and it is the discipline this post
is trying to hold in §11.3.

### 12.4 The corpus's own limits

The corpus is not complete, and it says so. The 14 November entry ends with a "Known residuals"
paragraph:

> **Known residuals:** Some absorbed member/internal tests and probes may still depend on
> environment-specific deps or pre-existing API mismatches not addressed in this pass; generated
> inventories/bench docs beyond sitemap and absorption docs may retain historical references. Gateway
> `SUBSYSTEMS` registry still contains entries for absorbed standalone subsystems (names preserved;
> implementations resolved via absorbed packages where applicable).

That is a list of things that are probably still wrong, written by the agent that did the work, in
the document that records what it did. It is the most useful kind of entry in the corpus and the
hardest to write.
