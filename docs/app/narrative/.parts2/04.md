The subject is false.

### 11.3 What the check-runs say

The parent of `7ba10cf4` is `987c9782` (*"comics: THE Z CORPS #10 — The Four-Channel Claim (38pp)"*),
and its check-runs are:

```
--- 987c9782 ---
total 14 Counter({'success': 9, 'failure': 5})
RED: ['C++ surface guard', 'artifact evidence', 'controlz desktop wiring',
      'mem20agentz OREO integration', 'Tests']
```

Five red, not four. The subject says "all four red checks," and the parent had five.

And `7ba10cf4` itself:

```
--- 7ba10cf4 ---
total 14 Counter({'success': 11, 'failure': 3})
RED: ['mem20agentz OREO integration', 'controlz desktop wiring', 'artifact evidence']
```

Three red. The commit took five red checks to three, which is a real improvement and a real piece of
work. It did not make them green.

The next commit, `105b9602` (*"fix CI: regenerate the two web lockfiles so npm ci resolves"*), takes
it to one:

```
=== TODAY CHECK RUNS on HEAD (105b9602) ===
total 16 Counter({'success': 15, 'failure': 1})
failure | artifact evidence
```

Fifteen of sixteen green, one red. The one red is `artifact evidence`, and its check-run carries no
output summary:

```
artifact evidence 136903384930 None |
```

So the window ends with a repository that is one check away from release-clean, and a commit in the
middle of the last day that claimed to have finished the job three checks early.

> **What broke.** Commit `7ba10cf4` is titled *"make all four red checks green on main."* Its parent
> had five red checks; the commit left three. The subject overstates the result by three checks, and
> the overstatement is in the one field a reader is most likely to trust without checking.
>
> **What we learned.** This is the same defect as the publication note in §1, in a different medium.
> There, a find-and-replace made a dated record state a false version. Here, a commit subject states
> a result the commit did not achieve. Both are claims that a reader would take at face value, and
> both are wrong in the direction of looking better. The project's own standard — *"A partial pass is
> not a clean pass"* — is exactly the standard this subject violates: five red to three red is a
> partial pass, and calling it "all green" is the failure the standard exists to prevent. **The
> subject line is a claim, and a claim about a check must be written after the check has run, not
> before.** The body of `7ba10cf4` is exemplary; the subject is the only part of it that is wrong, and
> it is the part that will be read.

### 11.4 The artifact-evidence check

The one remaining red check is worth a paragraph, because it is the check that has been red the
longest. It appears in the 16 November program status as one of three reds:

> | `artifact evidence` | 8 | [43820093456](...) |

Eight annotations. And it appears in the 1 November scoreboard's list of passing checks, which means
it was green then and is red now. The failures corpus records the rule it enforces, from 2 November:

> `85cee574|fix(ci): exempt convergence merges and gate-own changes from artifact-evidence`

So the check requires every PR to carry a 40-character SHA or a 200-verified URL, and it has been
patched at least once to exempt convergence merges. It is red at the end of the window with eight
annotations and no summary, which means the check is failing and the check-run does not say why in a
form the API exposes.

This post cannot determine the cause. What it can say is that the check that enforces the program's
central evidence rule is itself failing, and that the failure is not diagnosable from the check-run
output. That is a real limit, and §13 records it as one.

Part two §9 measured the corpus and found that its newest entry was dated 2026-09-29 — a month before

### 12.2 The entries that are not failures

Three entries in the window are recorded as non-failures, and the distinction is worth preserving

The second entry is the important one. The agent created a directory, found an existing service
already running, and recorded that the service was **pre-existing and not created this session** —
*"to avoid misattribution."* That is an agent declining credit for work it did not do, in a document
whose only reader is the next agent. It is the single best example in the repository of the culture
the chronicle is trying to describe.

*"Explicitly not claimed as fully closed"* is the sentence. An issue was fixed for the cases it
named and the fix declined to claim the whole issue, because part of it was separate work. That is
the same discipline as the roster score's two partial deliveries, and it is the discipline this post

That is a list of things that are probably still wrong, written by the agent that did the work, in
the document that records what it did. It is the most useful kind of entry in the corpus and the

## 14. Standing of the window

The window is the largest in the serial and the most mixed.

**What improved, measurably.** The red `Tests` check that part two spent a section on was fixed, and
fixed correctly — by substituting the successor package rather than deleting the phantom name, which
kept the cardinality contract and gained thirteen tests. The C++ engine was restored to the
repository and 279 tests pass. The launch page now states the measured surface instead of deferring
it. The tool count converged on a value that two independent catalogs agree on. Seventy-nine issues
were closed. The branch count fell from 103 to 2. The failures corpus was extended from a month stale
to current.

**What did not improve.** There is still no check that release tags are monotonic in `VERSION`. There
is still no path exclusion preventing sweeps from rewriting dated records — and the absence cost this
chronicle two of its own delivered artefacts. The house number (257/26) was 230 tools and 103 domains
away from the served surface and was corrected only when someone wrote it down as a blocker. Nineteen
tools are registered and unwired. The benchmark rows that would show where retrieval fails are still
unscored, and the two agents assigned to them produced nothing. The `artifact evidence` check is red
and undiagnosable from its own output.

**The composite read.** The program is one check away from release-clean and several controls away
from being able to stay there. The controls it lacks are all the same kind: a check that runs at the
moment of the operation rather than at the moment of the audit. The tag monotonicity check, the
dated-record exclusion, the branch-deletion ancestry gate, the "publish the grep" rule — every one of
them is a check that would have caught a defect in this window, and every one of them exists as a
written rule rather than as a gate.

That is the honest standing. The window contains a great deal of real work, done to a written plan,
verified with commands, and recorded with unusual candour. It also contains a repository-wide
find-and-replace that falsified a dated record, a commit subject that claimed a result it did not
achieve, and fifty commits that were catalogued as at-risk and then disappeared. Both things are true
of the same twenty days, and a chronicle that reported only one of them would be a worse document
than either.

### The thread this post leaves open

Part two left a question: does a check exist that `VERSION` is monotonic across release tags? The
answer is still no, and the window added a second question of the same shape: does a check exist that
prevents a sweep from rewriting a dated record? The answer is also no, and the evidence is §1.

The next instalment should answer whether either check was built. If neither was, the honest thing to
write is that the program has now recorded the same class of defect three times — the tag inversion,
the archive rewrite, and the commit subject — and has not yet built the gate that would catch any of
them.
