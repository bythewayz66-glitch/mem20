The audit: every delivered document against its publication commit

2026-11-21 grounded 1279 words
All installments RSS

Origin: grounded. This installment reports what the repository actually did in its window. Every figure in it was measured against the tree, and the chapter names the file or command it came from. Where it could not stand behind something, it says so in its own text rather than smoothing it over.

The previous instalment ended with a list of things it could not verify. One of them was whether the rewrites it had found were isolated or representative. This section answers that, by running the check that does not exist as a gate — by hand, once, across every delivered document in the repository.

5.1 The method

For each delivered document:

first=$(git log --diff-filter=A --format='%H' -- "$f" | tail -1)
git diff "$first" HEAD -- "$f"

--diff-filter=A finds the commit that added the file — its publication. tail -1 takes the earliest such commit, in case a file was deleted and re-added. The diff then compares the file as it stands at main against the version at publication.

Thirty-one documents were audited: five serial instalments, two program-history documents, eleven long-form copy assets, ten daily reports, the checkpoints ledger, and published/README.md.

Five retroactive rewrites. The previous instalment found two. The audit found three more, all in the daily reports, all from the same 18 November sweep.

5.3 The three the previous instalment missed

published/daily-reports/2026-11-01.md — added c2180055 (2026-11-01T22:27:10Z), rewritten by d47c75ac and 0cd80344, 7 insertions and 7 deletions.

The report's "Repo health" table now states VERSION | 4.0.0 for a measurement taken on 1 November. Its "Latest tag" row now reads v4.0.0 @ 48c241a1 where the tag at that commit is v3.1.2. And its "Verified in this run" paragraph now says VERSION 4.0.0 and v4.0.0 @ 48c241a1, ancestor of main, 83 behind.

The d47c75ac leg did something different: it replaced a branch name in the agent-attribution table with mem20pipez. That is the same consolidation sweep that rewrote part two's reproduction script.

published/daily-reports/2026-11-02.md — added 2b7f22ff (2026-11-02T08:16:58-07:00), rewritten by 0cd80344, 2 insertions and 2 deletions.

Two lines. VERSION 3.1.2 → 4.0.0 in the repo-health line, and v3.1.2 = 48c241a1, 296 commits behind → v4.0.0 = 48c241a1, 296 commits behind.

The second is the more interesting one, because the rewrite has made the report assert something internally impossible. A tag named v4.0.0 pointing at 48c241a1 is exactly the defect the report was describing — a tag that is not at HEAD. The rewrite did not fix the defect; it renamed the tag in the description of the defect, so the report now says "v4.0.0 is 296 commits behind main" about a commit that never carried that tag.

published/daily-reports/2026-11-14.md — added 85186b5a (2026-11-14T08:07:46-07:00), rewritten by 0cd80344, 6 insertions and 6 deletions.

Six lines: VERSION 3.1.2 → 4.0.0, Latest semver tag v3.1.2 → v4.0.0, v3.1.2 is 660 commits behind → v4.0.0 is 660 commits behind, and the same substitutions in the verified list and the action table.

This one compounds. The 14 November report was written after the 9 November sweep and before the 18 November one. It was accurate when written. It was then falsified four days later by a sweep that had nothing to do with it.

5.4 What a legitimate correction looks like

The audit found four, and they are worth stating because the distinction is the whole point.

published/history/mem20-program-history.md (added c69b1fb0, changed by de37968a, 2 insertions and 1 deletion) had a quoted rule changed from "Sidney is not a storage location" to "Sidney is retired and is not a storage location", with a parenthetical noting the host and publish gate no longer exist.

That is a correction. The change does not alter what the history says happened. It updates a rule that is still in force, in a document that is a living statement of policy rather than a dated transcript. The original sentence remains true; the new one is more true, and it is dated by its commit.

published/history/README.md (added 69507f2a, changed by de37968a, 1 insertion and 1 deletion) got the same treatment to the same rule.

published/README.md (added 4375f77c, changed by five commits, 30 insertions and 8 deletions) is a living index. a80fd259 repaired a truncated write of MANIFEST.tsv; 2960e662 updated a stale 257-tool claim to 268; de37968a purged retired host references. Each change is dated by its commit and none removes a claim a reader could have verified.

published/content/mem20-long-form-v3/launch-copy-pack.md (added b17d5f84, 9 insertions and 2 deletions) was published as a three-line placeholder and later filled in with the real pack. A placeholder replaced by its content is not a rewrite of a record; the placeholder asserted nothing that the replacement contradicts.

5.5 The test that separates them

The test is not "was the file edited". It is: can a reader still reconstruct what was true at the moment the document claims to describe?

A correction adds information and leaves the old claim standing, dated. A rewrite removes the old claim and puts a new one in its place, undated, so that the document now asserts something that was never true when it says it was.

By that test, the four corrections above pass and the five rewrites fail. The publication note fails because its printed transcript no longer matches what the printed commands return. Part two fails because its reproduction script no longer runs. The three daily reports fail because their tables of figures no longer describe the dates in their filenames.

5.6 The append-only addition

One document was extended without being edited: published/daily-reports/2026-10-31.md (added f25b57d7, changed by 4f20ecfb, 10 insertions and 0 deletions). A "Credential attestation" section was appended at the end. No existing line changed.

That is the shape a correction to a dated record should take. The record is extended, not edited. A reader can still see what the report said on 31 October, and can also see what was added to it later.

5.7 The assembly

docs/app/narrative/serial-2026-11-21.md (added b5107c53, changed by five assembly commits, 1,273 insertions and 0 deletions) is not an edit at all. No line was ever deleted. The intermediate word counts — 4,903, 20,009, 8,066, 8,199, 16,264, 16,266 — are artefacts of partial assembly, not of content being removed.

The 20,009-word intermediate is the one that needs explaining, and §2.6 explains it: the first assembly concatenated a parts directory containing duplicated sections. Its heading list has 21 entries with four duplicates; the final has 17 with none.

What broke. The previous instalment found two rewrites and reported them as the finding. There were five. The three it missed were in a directory it had not thought to audit — published/daily-reports/ — because the two it had found were in docs/app/narrative/, and it generalised from the directory it happened to be looking at.

What we learned. A finding is not a survey. Finding two instances of a defect in one directory is evidence that the defect exists, not evidence about its extent. The extent has to be measured, and the measurement is mechanical: enumerate the population, run the same check on every member, and report the count. The previous instalment should have done that before publishing, and this one had to do it after.
